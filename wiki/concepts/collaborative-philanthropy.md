# Collaborative Philanthropy

How ShikshaLokam thinks about funding — and about funders. The short version: **giving alone doesn't
move a system; orchestration does.** This page gathers what the brain already holds, scattered across
the strategy note, the InvokED corpus, and the external evidence, into one place.

## The thesis in one line

Philanthropy that funds isolated projects buys outputs. Philanthropy that funds **leadership,
coherence, and long-term institutional capacity** — alongside government, community, markets, and
media — builds a system that keeps improving after the grant ends.

## Where this sits in SL's own strategy

From [[shikshalokam-2-strategy-note-jul2025]] — the canonical SL-org positioning document:

- **Fund Orchestration** is one of six new capabilities SL 2.0 must add, defined as working across
  *HNIs + institutions + philanthropies* — not a single donor relationship, a portfolio of them.
- It is a standing internal function: the **Funding Orchestration Team** sits alongside System
  Orchestration, Ecosystem Development, and Narrative Building.
- SL 2.0's first role is **System Orchestrator** — *"facilitating meaningful and impactful collective
  action … developing scalable frameworks, platforms, and initiatives … aligned toward common goals."*
- The note is explicit that this is a precondition, not a nice-to-have:
  > *"To catalyse both national and global conversations around education leadership and systemic
  > school improvements, it will require the involvement of diverse philanthropic institutions and
  > key stakeholders from the outset."*
- **Fund** is one of the **10 verbs of the movement** — sitting level with Amplify, Convene,
  Orchestrate, Mentor, Build Narrative. Money is one contribution among ten, not the apex one.

## The roles funders actually play

The strategy note's ecosystem map already distinguishes kinds of funding relationship — use these
names rather than the generic "donor":

- **Strategic Partners** — long-term funding + tech + knowledge. *Named: Axis Bank, AWS, Societal Thinking.*
- **Anchor Partners** — sub-collective drivers whose founders commit to being Weavers. *Named: Commutiny.*
- **Momentum Partners** — on-ground change initiatives. *Named: Vidhya Vidhai (TN), Jyothi Mahila Samakhya (Bihar).*
- **Co-Builders** — shaping and anchoring development in a geography (SL Foundation itself, in India).
- **Advisors / Mentors / Ambassadors** — counsel, field support, and mainstream visibility.

Founder/patron context: **SFPI** is the founding philanthropy (ShikshaLokam est. 2017; SL Foundation's
operational costs covered by SFPI), and remains the only named funder of SL itself — see
[[ecosystem]]. The Shibulal family's philanthropic journey began in **1998** with a scholarship scheme
in S.D. Shibulal's hometown in Kerala.

## Capital beyond money

The sharpest language the brain holds comes out of InvokED 5.0 ([[invoked-5-proceedings-2026]]), where
a whole Brain Date was built on this: **"Beyond Fundraising — Orchestrating Capital."** The running
theme across the convening was **capital beyond money — trust, networks, relationships.**

The approved caption for that session ([[../voice/exemplars/invoked-session-teaser-2026]]) is the
cleanest public expression we have:

> What if the most valuable capital for systems change isn't money—but trust?
>
> … Lakshmi Pattabi Raman (Chief of Fund Orchestration at Centre for Exponential Change) … and
> Manisha Shah Nayyar (Philanthropy Partner for Asia at Synergos) explored how scaling social impact
> requires **moving beyond fundraising to orchestrating relationships, knowledge, governance, and
> shared ownership.**
>
> The conversation reflected on designing for collaboration, building trust across funders and
> practitioners, investing in long-term partnerships, and creating systems that allow **different
> forms of capital to work together** toward lasting change.

## The three questions we put to funders

From the closed-door **Strategic Partners Roundtable** invite ([[invoked-5-email-invites-2026]] § E),
signed by S.D. Shibulal. These are the canonical framing questions — reuse them verbatim:

1. How can funding shift from **isolated projects** to strengthening **leadership, coherence, and
   long-term institutional capacity**?
2. What role can funders play in **building bridges across Sarkaar, Samaaj, Bazaar, and Sanchar** —
   to spark **movements rather than programmes**?
3. What does it take to create the conditions, partnerships, and enabling environment for a national
   movement like Shikshagraha to thrive?

The framing of the room matters too: *"an exclusive gathering of funders and movement-builders
committed to accelerating systemic change"* — funders are convened **as movement-builders**, not as a
cheque-writing audience. Same logic in the InvokED 5.0 concept note ([[invoked-5-concept-note-2026]]),
whose stated aim is to *"deepen collaboration among co-creators — government, civil society, and
philanthropic partners."*

## The evidence we can cite

**External validation** — [[research-wef-future-is-collective-2025]] (Schwab Foundation / World
Economic Forum, *The Future is Collective*, 2025). Tier 1. It highlights case studies where **funders
enabled platforms and networks** rather than projects, naming MapBiomas and Shikshagraha, and names
**SFPI** as enabling collective action in India *"by weaving Shikshagraha."*

> ⚠ That source carries a `verify_before_publish` flag — the in-report naming of *Shikshagraha* needs
> eyes on the PDF before any published piece cites it. Check the source file first.

**Our own proof** — [[shikshalokam-brazil-stories-may2026]], slide-headed *"The Transformative Power of
Collective Action: Evidence from the ground."* Each of the four stories names its collective,
including who funded:

- **Punjab Education Collective** — Dept. of School Education + Sanjhi Sikhya + Samarthya +
  Mantra4Change + ShikshaLokam, funded by **Deloitte and A.T.E. Chandra Foundation**. Punjab moved
  **13th → 1st** in national school rankings in two years, and held it for five (corroborated
  independently by the WEF report).
- **Nagaland PBL** — Dept. of School Education & Samagra Shiksha + **Education Above All** (content
  design *and* funding) + NagaED + Mantra4Change + ShikshaLokam. Collective action produced *"quicker
  orchestration; informed program design leveraging cross-state evidence."*

The pattern worth naming: in both, the funder is **inside** the collective with a role, not outside it
with a reporting requirement.

## How to write about it

- **Lead with the shift, not the ask.** Isolated projects → leadership and capacity. Programmes →
  movements. Fundraising → orchestration.
- **Money is one of ten verbs.** Never imply funding is the senior contribution.
- **Name the collective.** Our credibility on this comes from being able to list who did what — see
  the Brazil stories template.
- **Use "capital beyond money"** — trust, networks, relationships, knowledge, governance, shared
  ownership. It's ours and it's earned.
- **Drop movement stats for global philanthropy audiences.** Per `learnings/2026-06-01-vip-invite-voice.md`,
  "1.2M micro-improvements" and Shikshagraha framing don't land with global partners — let the
  InvokED context carry it. "Cherished" for long-standing civil-society partners; "valuable" for
  corporate ones.
- **Tone for long-form donor content is an open gap** in [[../voice/styleguide]] — only blog and web
  copy were captured. Draft, then get it validated.
