# Our Samvaad — reflecting on the movement

An org-wide **Samvaad**: 11 breakout rooms, each answering the same four prompts about a national
student-led education movement (a paper-leak and examination-integrity protest that grew to national
scale, drew cross-generational support, and ended with the education minister resigning and a reform
committee being announced).

**This is internal team sensemaking, not an organisational position.** Nothing here has been
adopted as SL's stance. Individual contributors are not named in this file by design. Treat every
line as *"someone in the room said this"* — never as *"ShikshaLokam believes."*

The four prompts: **What did we see/hear/observe · What assumptions got challenged · What tensions
are we holding · What new possibilities are we seeking, for individuals and the organisation.**

## What people saw

- **Solidarity across every line.** Age, caste, class, religion, geography. Repeatedly noted as the
  defining feature. *"Not just a GenZ issue. Education is a concern across generations."*
- **Joy as method.** Singing, dancing, humour, creativity. *"Brutality was there, but the whole
  thing was turned into fun and humour. The messaging was not one of doom."* Several rooms named
  this as what made it durable and safe to join.
- **Distributed participation.** *"People found their own places and role in the movement. Not
  everyone was on the front foot, but support was shown in multiple ways."*
- **Education became a political agenda** — several rooms flagged this as the genuinely unusual
  thing, given that political debate is usually identity-based.
- **A split media picture.** Traditional TV read as building a narrative against the students;
  independent and social media read as carrying the real one. *"Many protests happen at Jantar
  Mantar, but this one managed to go outside the physical space. It connected emotionally."*
- Clear, narrow, repeatable demands. And *"needed one person to start."*

## Assumptions that got challenged

The most useful column in the sheet. Nearly every room listed a belief that broke:
- **That protests fade.** This one did not.
- **That the government would not budge.** It did — though several rooms immediately asked *why*,
  and whether elections explain it.
- **That young people are politically apathetic**, and that quiet people are content with the
  status quo.
- **That online activism cannot produce real-world change.**
- **That education change comes only through policy.** Replaced with: collective action can move
  policy directly.
- **That protest must be angry and grim** to be serious.
- One room noted the absence that no one else did: **thousands of education NGOs in the country,
  and almost none of them said anything** — for or against.

## Tensions being held

- **Is a resignation a result?** Widely doubted. *"So what if the minister resigned? Will it stop
  paper leaks?"* Concern about tokenism, and about one big step drawing attention away from the
  systemic problem.
- **Where does accountability actually sit** — one minister, or bureaucrats, systems and the wider
  chain too?
- **Positioning risk for an organisation that works with government.** Named plainly in more than
  one room: fear that being visible at a protest site puts the organisation in a negative light and
  jeopardises its system relationships.
- **Selective solidarity.** *"Why is the same solidarity not visible for Manipur, or for tribal
  protests happening now?"*
- **What the movement carried with it.** One room did not appreciate the ableism, misogyny,
  classism and transphobia that surfaced in places.
- **Narrow issue vs whole system.** The movement was about one examination; caste, gender and class
  discrimination in schools stayed untouched. Will those conversations continue?
- **Reform committee composition** questioned — technology-led, when the surfaced problems were not
  only technological.
- **From outrage to work.** *"What happens now, when the work needs to be done? How will
  differences be resolved when such diverse perspectives are present? What does resolution even
  look like?"*

## New possibilities named

Grouped, because the same ideas recurred across rooms:

**Take a position.**
*"Organisations and NGOs should take a stand. They cannot be neutral. There is nothing like a
neutral political stand."* Counterweighted by the positioning-risk tension above — the two sit
unresolved side by side, and that is the honest state of the conversation.

**Use the moment for Shikshagraha.**
Education has public attention for the first time in a long while. Several rooms asked whether the
movement can be a catalyst — extending examination reform into school-level reform, and using the
open attention to show people the larger Shikshagraha vision.

**Surface and back youth voices.**
*"Young people as movement leaders, not just beneficiaries or protesters."* Plus: a responsibility
to listen carefully rather than react, and to figure out how the organisation actually surfaces
those voices.

**Demand-side work, alongside system work.**
Working with communities on demand creation. Making **Chaupals** places where people are encouraged
to question at local level. One room floated separate organisational identities so that partnership
with government and pressure on government need not compete — noted here as an idea raised, not a
recommendation.

**Distributed, joyful movement-building.** *"How can movement building be more decentralised and
joyful?"* Digital platforms as spaces for civic participation, not just awareness.

**Comms and influence.** Working with social-media voices aligned to the mission; carrying the
bigger picture through comms; a considered political engagement strategy for education.

**The personal is political.** *"How do we as individuals show up more honestly at work and for the
movement? How might we create space for the same?"* Bringing evidence into conversations at home,
in families and friend groups.

## Why this matters to the brain

- It is the clearest existing record of **how this team actually thinks about collective action** —
  which is the core claim of [[shikshagraha-narrative-2026]] and of most InvokED messaging. Useful
  as substance, not as quotable copy.
- It contains a live, unresolved strategic question — **can an organisation that works inside
  government also apply pressure on it** — that will surface again in positioning work.
- **Do not quote it outward.** It is candid internal reflection, includes political commentary
  about named public figures and parties, and was written for colleagues in a closed room. Anything
  drawn from it for external use needs Sonal's explicit yes first.
