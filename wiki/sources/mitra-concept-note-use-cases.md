# MItra — the four use cases

The canonical answer to *"what is MItra used for."* Taken from the **MItra Concept Note**, which
carries two tabs: **Version 1 (2025)** and **Version 2 (24 June 2026)**. Version 2 is current and
rebrands the capability **"MItra by ELEVATE."** A near-identical Comms cut exists
([MItra Concept Note <> Comms](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1eQ1ynupDJrZVI0XjeCu3RUv4EGq_ClqYi85qa_c6maY/edit))
and agrees with V2 on all four.

## What MItra is

**AI-powered · multilingual · voice-enabled · open-source.** A companion that listens to voices from
the ground and turns them into structured knowledge **without asking users to change how they
communicate**. A lightweight progressive web app, works on low bandwidth and mobile data.

The journey: *a leader speaks in their local dialect → MItra listens, transcribes, segments
challenges from solutions, prompts for gaps → structured data flows into reports, dashboards and a
shared knowledge base.*

Built specifically for people who have **little to no literacy**, limited digital exposure, work in
**low-connectivity** settings, speak **local languages or dialects**, and are *"closest to the
problem but furthest from the systems designed to solve it."*

Anchor line, used everywhere: **"Not a survey. Not a form. A companion."**

## THE FOUR USE CASES (Version 2 — current)

MItra is described as **"not a single-use tool… a flexible listening infrastructure."**

**1. Discussion Capture**
Voice-enabled bots listen to and structure **group conversations** — community meetings, chaupals,
chavadis — capturing challenges, solutions and action points in real time, in regional language.
*Live in:* **Shiksha Chaupal / Chavadi**, where women leaders share educational challenges in their
communities. Surfaces parental concerns, gender-based educational barriers and school-level
structural gaps. Aggregation across geographies is the point — recurring themes and systemic gaps.

**2. Story Capture**
Bots guide an individual — community member, teacher, any changemaker — through telling a **story of
change**, turning a personal narrative into structured, shareable knowledge. Four named mechanics:
- **Guided Reflection** — step-by-step prompts on challenges faced, actions taken, time invested,
  impact achieved.
- **Evidence Collection** — photos of the work attach to the story and land in the final report.
- **Structured Output** — a professionally formatted written story capturing problem, actions,
  results, so the *"real effort"* behind an improvement is legible.
- **Review and Edit** — the user reviews and corrects before it's finalised.

**3. Evaluation Studies**
Structured bots run **assessments, baseline and endline studies, validations and programme
evaluations** — replacing expensive, logistics-heavy survey operations with a lightweight voice-first
alternative. *Deployed across* teachers, students, government officials and community members for
**FGDs and interviews** in large-scale evaluation studies. School leaders have also used it to
reflect on their own improvement journeys, validating programme hypotheses at scale.

**4. Perception or Feedback Capture**
Conversational bots surface **what people think, feel and believe** about their schools, communities
and their children's futures. A parent names what enables or blocks their child's education; a
community member says what future readiness or inclusion means to them. *Live in:* **PTM feedback
with parents**, **community voices on inclusion and future readiness at Shiksha Samvaad convenings**,
and **large-scale parent perception studies running across states**.

**Beyond these four:** the note is explicit that the same capability works across sectors — farmers,
health workers, youth and community groups — anywhere improvements and discussions need capturing
through voice.

## Version 1 (2025) — superseded, do not use

The older tab lists a different four. Included only so nobody re-quotes them from an old deck:
**Capturing Micro-Improvements · Community Voices · Feedback Collection · Repository of
Improvements**. Note that **Repository of Improvements** — a growing best-practice base where AI
matches a user's current challenge to a relevant exemplar project they can adapt and execute — has
**no direct successor** in V2's four; in V2 it survives only as a *benefit* ("Scalable Knowledge
Sharing"), not a use case. Worth confirming whether that was intentional.

## The six benefits (V1 framing, still quoted)

Visibility at Scale · System-wide Sensemaking · Amplification of Grassroots Voices · Faster Programme
Insights · Guided Action and Replicability · Scalable Knowledge Sharing. V2 compresses these to four
by dropping *Faster Programme Insights* and *Guided Action* and adding **Inclusive Decision-Making**.

## Deployments visible in Drive (the requirement templates)

Every deployment starts from **`MItra - Product Requirement Template`** — tabs for Overview, a
Question Bank, Dashboard_Metrics and Report_Metrics/Templates. Confirmed live copies:
- **Shiksha Samvaad** (`MItra-New Requirement_Shiksha Samvaad`) — includes a **Skoll Convening**
  overview variant; question bank at V3, English + Hindi + Tamil.
- **PPPI Pilot** — parent perception study, Hindi and Kannada, two question sets, district-level
  segmentation across Karnataka. Dashboard metrics map each metric to a specific question number
  (e.g. *School Visit Rate ← "How often do you visit your child's school?"*).
- **WLC** (in the blank template) — gender-responsive classroom practices; metric *"No of discussions
  recorded as part of WLC."*
- **InvokED Impact Survey** — see [[../../projects/invoked-impact-survey/page]]. Use case 3 + 4 in
  combination.
