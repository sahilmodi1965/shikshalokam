## The film & the recognition
- *Mothers of Courage* — an **8-minute documentary** following **first-generation-literate mothers in
  rural Bihar** who take a stand against **child marriage** and ensure their **daughters stay in school**
  longer.
- **India's only shortlisted entry in the Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) category at the Cannes
  Lions** — framed as the first Indian entry in that category. *(Verify exact award wording vs the brain's
  other note that it won Silver in the ABBY Awards SDG category — these are different awards; keep them
  distinct.)*
- Anchor's framing: *"the most powerful stories don't come from the glam and the glitz and big stages,
  but from the courtyards of rural India."*

## Khushboo Awasthi — key points
- On the shortlist news, her **first thought wasn't about Cannes — it was about the mothers/women in
  Bihar.**
- In the chaupals, women have shared they **don't believe their voices matter** — all their lives,
  decisions were made by others for them and the girls in the family.
- Over the chaupals, the **conversation shifted**: women started asking questions, identifying problems,
  **taking ownership**, and **believing in their own agency** to challenge norms.
- The film was made primarily to **spread awareness** among other women and community members — that their
  voices matter, and change comes only with commitment and action.
- **Urban–rural / aspiration myth:** there was a time a girl finishing school was "extraordinary"; today
  even the most disadvantaged parents feel girls have an equal right to education. In the **last decade she
  has not met a single parent** who said education isn't necessary for their child. **It's not lack of
  aspiration — it's systemic barriers:** schools far with no transport; both parents working with younger
  siblings to mind; guests arriving needing cooking; no awareness of what to do after class 10; graduates
  without jobs.
- Breaking these barriers needs **all actors — government, society, community, civil society — together.**

## Khushboo on Shikshagraha (the movement)
- **Narrative building is one of the most important aspects of movement building.**
- Named **Shikshagraha** deliberately — *"not a program, not a campaign — a movement."* As people came
  together for freedom through **Satyagraha**, can we come together against **education inequity** →
  **Shikshagraha.**
- Already **powerful local partners** in the system doing great work — Urmila Didi and her network, and
  others across **Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Telangana, Tamil Nadu.**
- **"Only when there is collective voice and collective action does the narrative become strong enough for
  even government to take notice and join hands."**
- **The power of media = amplifying stories of hope.** *"Stories matter because they show you things have
  changed, things are changing, and people have hope — they have not given up, and so we should not give
  up."*

## Urmila Didi — key points
- Pride/joy that Bihar women's voices — especially remote/village — are being carried far and appreciated.
- Her journey began in the **1974 JP Movement**; **jailed as a young woman activist.**
- Earlier, women would say *"talk to my **malik** (master/owner)"* — the husband seen as owner. Through
  years of chaupal work, **"at least he's not the malik now — he's a partner, a life partner."**
- Now women speak for their daughters, grandmothers for granddaughters — worried about their **education.**
- **Story (Muzaffarpur · Bandra block · village Mutlupur):** a class-10 girl about to drop out; seeing the
  girl's "flutter to keep studying," her mother chose to educate her but had no money — **sold a goat
  (worth ₹3,000, sold for ₹2,400) to fill the ₹3,000 exam form.** A **motivational song** in the chaupal
  moved a mother to tears; with one day left, she **sold her goat to fill her daughter's form.**
- The **transformation:** mothers who once convinced themselves and their daughters that study "wasn't
  needed" now say *"my daughter will study,"* and daughters say *"I want to study, not marry now."*
- **Her dream:** the chaupals (now run for education/rights/against child marriage) will **change form —
  women independently running their own chaupals in their villages, talking about village development.**

## The throughline
- **It has to be a collective effort** — government, society, community, civil society, together.
- Media's role is to **amplify stories of hope**; stories matter because they prove change is possible.
