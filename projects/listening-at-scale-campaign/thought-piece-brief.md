# Thought-piece brief — Leadership byline #1

A brief for comms to write ONE leadership thought piece. Comms drafts; a leader is the byline.

> 🚫 **No numbers.** Our impact figures aren't MItra voice-captures and aren't segregated by future
> readiness — so this piece claims **no counts**. Its authority comes from the question, the named
> institutional proof (Bihar), and real voices. See the no-numbers rule on [[page]].

## At a glance
- **Working title:** *"Who Gets to Decide What 'Future-Ready' Means?"*
  - Alt: *"We Are Preparing Children for a Future We Never Asked Them About."*
  - Alt: *"Not a Survey. A Companion — and What It's Telling Us."*
- **Theme (one idea):** India is building toward Viksit Bharat 2047, and the generation that has to
  deliver it is in school right now — yet "future-ready" is being defined in rooms without the
  teachers, parents, students and communities raising them. Listening at scale changes *who holds the
  pen.*
- **Author / leader:** **Neeraj Doddamane (COO)** — systems-change, sense-making register. *TBC.*
  - Alt: **Khushboo Awasthi (Chief Curator, Shikshagraha)** if the angle leans more community/movement.
- **Audience:** Ecosystem leaders, funders, partner networks (SLC/YLC/WLC, Mantra4Change), and state
  officials — people who make or fund decisions about what "future ready" schooling means.
- **Length / format:** ~900–1,200 words. LinkedIn long-form + newsletter block. This is the
  **campaign-opening piece — Phase 1 (Aug–Sep)** of the Aug 2026 → InvokED 6.0 arc. It plants the
  question the whole campaign then collects answers to.

## Flow / outline (6 beats)

1. **The hook — a scattered treasure.** Open on the image: India's 1.5 lakh schools already hold the
   fixes we're looking for, but *"treasure scattered across the country, and no map to find it."*
   The best answers to future readiness already exist on the ground — we just can't see them.

2. **The quiet failure.** We decide the future of learning in rooms full of experts, and leave out the
   teacher, the school leader, the parent. That means *"taking decisions based on incomplete
   information."* Not for lack of care — for lack of a way to hear at scale.

3. **What listening at scale actually means.** Reframe: this isn't data collection. *"Not a survey.
   Not a form. A companion."* MItra — our voice-enabled, multilingual AI listening tool — *"talks like
   a friend, not a never-ending questionnaire."* The test we hold it to: *"Does this feel human?"*

4. **The proof it's real — the spread, not a score.** We're not describing an intention; it's
   happening. Show it with **range**: a teacher in Nagaland, a mother in Bihar, a student in
   Karnataka, a headteacher in Villupuram — conversations held in Hindi, Kannada, Telugu, across
   chaupals and field Samvaads. *(Cite: [[../../wiki/sources/listening-at-scale-corpus-2026]].)*
   **No counts, no totals, no "million".** Geography and language carry the scale; a single
   specific voice carries more weight than any figure would.

5. **Where the system said yes — Bihar.** Listening at scale is no longer a side project: the
   **Department of Education, Government of Bihar** formalised it in an MoU — SL's role is to *connect
   the voices of parents, teachers and students to the system via an AI-based framework*, alongside six
   fellow organisations, at zero cost to the state. *(Cite:
   [[../../wiki/sources/bihar-education-dept-mou-2026]].)* When a state department builds listening into
   how it runs, listening becomes infrastructure.

6. **The close — the invitation, and the stage.** Land the thesis: future readiness should be defined
   by the people closest to each child, not decided over their heads. Then make the ask concrete —
   *we're asking three questions of children, teachers, parents and communities across the country,
   and what they say goes onto the InvokED 6.0 stage in February.* Answer it yourself. This is a
   movement anyone can listen into — and speak into.

## Voice guardrails
- Warm, plainspoken, hopeful. Systems-change register without jargon. Short sentences.
- Human stories only. **Zero statistics** — not ours, not borrowed. If a sentence needs a number to
  work, rewrite the sentence.
- Reuse the canonical lines verbatim — they're on-voice and already public.
- Named institutions (Bihar's Department of Education) are the credibility anchor in place of figures.
