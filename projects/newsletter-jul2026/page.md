## Current draft

Subject: A state chooses to listen, and Mothers of Courage keeps travelling...

Dear <<First Name>>,

Hope this message finds you in good spirits.

I am writing to you in this role for the first time, and I have been sitting with the question of what to say. What I keep returning to is something this mission taught me long before any title did: the change we are after does not begin in rooms like the one I am writing from. It begins with a mother in Bihar who decides her daughter's schooling comes before every other claim on the month. A teacher who stays back to ask why one child stopped coming. An officer who takes the slower decision because the evidence asked her to. A partner who sets aside their own logo so that a shared idea can travel further.

This month, the work of listening to those voices moved from something we believed in to something a state has formally asked for. Elsewhere, partners spent four days holding the movement's hardest questions, and a film made in rural Bihar kept finding new stages.

This edition is another window into the movement, in practice, across India.

Happy reading.

**A State That Chose to Listen: Bihar Signs for Its Schools**

The Department of Education, Government of Bihar has signed MoUs — at zero cost — with seven organisations: EkStep Foundation, Wadhwani AI, Khan Academy India, Pi Jam Foundation, Mantra4Change, Pratham Education Foundation and ShikshaLokam. Each brings one thing. Ours is listening. Through MItra, a friend-like, voice-enabled tool, the department will hear from parents, teachers and students in their own words and in their own language — while AI finds the patterns across the state without flattening the person who spoke. Seven organisations, one state, no money changing hands.

Read about the partnership: https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7483865650625433600

**Weavers in One Room: The Shikshagraha Convening**

Over four days, partner organisations across the Shikshagraha movement came together around the questions that don't have easy answers: how to strengthen public perception of government schools, how to make future readiness a national priority, how to orchestrate collective impact across districts, and how to use evidence to actually drive change. Parallel design and communications workshops asked how programme design and storytelling turn a shared vision into action. With 127 million children in India's public schools, no single organisation gets to solve this.

Read what came out of the convening: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/weavers-of-shikshagraha-ugcPost-7476173774288076800-gDXl/

**Listening at Scale (Part 2 of 3): The Journey of Building MItra**

Part 1 named the problem: a country sitting on grassroots wisdom with no good way to dig it out. Part 2 is about the harder moment — when someone stops saying *something should be done* and starts saying *we will*. Most tech products begin with a feature list; MItra began with a feeling. Built on generative AI in 2023, before it was obvious, by a team carrying lessons from DIKSHA and Karmayogi Bharat. Now piloted in Nagaland, Bihar and Karnataka, in Hindi, Kannada and Telugu.

Read how MItra was built: https://shikshalokam.org/listening-at-scale-part-2-of-3-journey-of-building-mitra/

Two more honours this month — the **Platinum SABRE (Best in Show)** and the **Gold SABRE for Public Education** at the SABRE South Asia Awards 2026, among the region's most prestigious communications awards.
