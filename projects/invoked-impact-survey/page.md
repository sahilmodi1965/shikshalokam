# InvokED Impact Survey — the plan

**What we're doing:** getting 150–250 senior education people to spend five minutes telling us what
happened for them *after* InvokED, and turning that into a report **by 30 September 2026**.

**The hard part is not the survey. It's getting people to answer.** We need 1 in every 4 or 5 people
from a list of about 900 — busy, senior people spread across the world. Send one email and about 70
answer. That's why this is a campaign and not a send.

---

| | **Half 1 — collect the answers** | **Half 2 — the report, and what it's used for** |

Half 1 is the whole game. Half 2 has nothing to say without it.

## 2 · Three things that decide whether people answer

### 2a · Don't call it a survey
That word means *form*, *extraction*, *ten minutes I don't have*. And it's not even true — the whole
point is that it's a conversation that asks a follow-up.

**Suggested name: "InvokED — What Happened Next."** It's the actual question, it's about them, and
it sounds like a story rather than a scale of 1 to 5.

Other options if you want a different feel:
- *"The InvokED Effect"* — punchier, but a bit pleased with itself.
- *"Five editions. What came of them?"* — the most honest, and the most open to a hard answer.
- *"What did InvokED set in motion?"* — lifts a line from the report itself.

**In everything we write, the format is the pitch:** *"Five minutes. A conversation, not a form.
Speak or type, whichever you prefer."*

### 2b · Give them something back — this is the gap in the build sheet
The sheet is very thorough about what we **get** (28 numbers, a report on every conversation, names
for the Awards) and says nothing about what the person answering **gets**. With this audience that
gap is the difference between 8 out of 100 answering and 25 out of 100. So we say plainly, in the
invite, what they get:

| | What they get | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The report before anyone else** — early access, ahead of the public release | Everyone who answers |
| 2 | **The "future readiness" findings** — how 150–250 senior education leaders define a phrase everybody uses and nobody defines the same way | Everyone who answers |
| 3 | Their story **featured**, their name in the report, considered for a **Shikshagraha Award**, invited to a **Forum** | If they say yes to it |

**Number 2 is our strongest card.** Question 8 isn't only our research question — it's a study of
their own field that they'd want to be part of. Lead with that, not with our impact measurement.
Nobody fills in a form so an organisation can prove its own worth. People do join a study of a
question they care about.

### 2c · Tell them a "no" is welcome
The build sheet's first honesty rule says a *no* on question 4 doesn't mean no impact. Say that **to
the person, in the invite**: *"If InvokED led to nothing for you, that's genuinely useful and we'd
still like to hear it."* That takes away the main reason someone with nothing to report would skip —
and those are exactly the people question 5 and the Forum list need.

## 3 · How people can answer — five ways, not one

The build sheet only plans the MItra bot. In practice people will answer in whatever way suits them,
and the most senior ones — the ones with the best stories — are the least likely to talk to a bot.
So we open five doors and take answers through all of them.

| How | Who it's for | Roughly how many | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MItra — speak or type** | Most people. The main route | 100–150 | The scale route. Asks the follow-up question by itself, which is where the good material comes from |
| **A short web form** | People who won't talk to a bot | 40–70 | Same 8 questions, trimmed. Cheap insurance, and it means we're not stuck if MItra is late |
| **Just reply to the email** | Senior people who'd rather write two paragraphs | 15–30 | Accept it. Don't send them back to a link. We code it in afterwards |
| **A 15-minute call** | The top ~30 — speakers, funders, government | 20–30 | Highest value by far. Best stories, and it's flattering rather than extractive |
| **In person** | Anyone we're already meeting between now and 20 Sep | 10–20 | Ask at the end of a meeting we're having anyway. Nearly free |

**With the top tier, offer a call, not a bot.** "Fifteen minutes with us" converts better than "use
our tool", and it produces the material the report actually needs.

**One thing this must not break: the numbers have to add up.** Whichever door someone comes through,
we capture the same core in the same way — editions attended (Q2), the shift (Q3), did something
substantial happen (Q4), and the ripple (Q5). Calls and email replies get typed into the same
structure straight after. Otherwise we end up with five piles that can't be added together.

**Good side effect:** this removes the single biggest risk in the earlier version of this plan. We
are no longer stuck waiting for the MItra bot to be ready. The form and the calls can start on time
regardless — MItra joins when it's built.

## 4 · The calendar

You're right that AI does the number-crunching fast, so the analysis window shrinks from two weeks
to about three days — and **that time goes back into collecting answers**. Half 1 gets a **fifth
round** and runs five weeks instead of four.

| **Thu 20 Aug** | **Round 2 — the wider send.** The remaining ~750, split by group, each from the right signer | Comms |
| **Mon 24 Aug** | **LinkedIn post 1** — "we're asking our alumni what happened next" | Comms |

| **Mon 31 Aug** | **Round 3 — reminder, and change the channel.** Email to those who haven't answered; WhatsApp (India) and LinkedIn messages (elsewhere), one to one from whoever knows them | Comms + relationship owners |
| **Mon 7 Sep** | **Round 4 — last call.** Email with the closing date, plus phone calls to the top 50 | Senders |
| **Mon 14 Sep** | **Round 5 — the new one.** Aimed only at the groups we're short of, and a personal nudge from Sonal to anyone who half-started | Comms |
| **Thu 17 Sep** | **LinkedIn post 2** — "closing Sunday", plus one early finding if we have a clean one | Comms |
| **Fri 18 Sep** | **Close** (weekend grace to Sun 20 Sep) | — |

| **Mon 28 – Tue 29 Sep** | **Review, design, Sonal's approval** | Sonal |

**One caution on the AI speed-up.** AI is genuinely fast at sorting answers into categories and
finding the patterns. It is not a substitute for judgement — picking the quotes, deciding what the
report claims, and honouring the five honesty rules still take a person. And **we must hand-check a
sample of about 30** against what the AI coded. If we skip that, we can't defend the numbers when a
funder asks, and this whole report exists to be believed. Two days is enough for both; two hours
isn't.

## 5 · Why five rounds — the arithmetic

Working from about 900 reachable people (probably 750–850 once the list is cleaned).

| Round | Who | Answer rate | Answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — personal, from a real person | 150 | 35% | ~52 |
| 2 — wider send | 750 | 8% | ~60 |
| 3 — reminder + WhatsApp/LinkedIn | ~840 left | 5% | ~42 |
| 4 — last call + phone the top 50 | ~800 | 4% | ~32 |
| 5 — targeted at thin groups | ~770 | 3% | ~23 |
| Calls + in person (running throughout) | 60 | 50% | ~30 |
| | | **Total** | **≈ 240** |

That's comfortably inside the 150–250 range — where four rounds landed at about 185. **The extra
week and the fifth round are worth roughly 55 more answers.** Good call.

**One email on its own gets about 70.** Worth saying out loud to anyone who suggests it.

**Watch the mix of people, not just the total.** 240 answers all from one kind of organisation is a
weaker report than 160 spread across funders, government, academics and edtech. The build sheet puts
it well: a good spread is what makes it believable. Check the mix every week and point rounds 4 and
5 at whoever's missing.

## 8 · What could go wrong

| Risk | How bad | What we do about it |
|---|---|---|
| **Contact list is patchy** — do we have current details for 800–1,000 people going back five editions? | 🔴 High. Now the biggest one | Audit it in prep week. Editions 1.0 and 2.0 will be the worst. Say the real reachable number before Round 1, not after |
| **Only one or two rounds actually go out** | 🔴 High | Rounds 3, 4 and 5 are nearly half the answers. Lock the dates and the owners now |
| **MItra bot isn't ready** | 🟠 Was critical, now medium — because the form and the calls don't depend on it | Still confirm the build date this week. If it slips, the form carries the volume |
| **Answers arrive in five different shapes and can't be added up** | 🟠 Medium | One common core across every route (Q2, Q3, Q4, Q5). Calls and email replies typed into the same structure the same day |
| **Wrong mix of people** | 🟠 Medium | Weekly check; aim rounds 4 and 5 at whoever's short |
| **AI mis-sorts the open answers** | 🟠 Medium | Hand-check a sample of 30. Non-negotiable — the report has to survive a funder reading it closely |
| **"Five minutes" isn't true** | 🟠 Medium | Time it honestly in prep week. If it's nine minutes, say nine. Overrunning a stated time with senior people costs us later |
| **Awards ask makes it feel like a transaction** | 🟠 Medium | Keep it optional and at the end. Never in the opening of the invite |
| **Too few answers for percentages to mean anything** | 🟠 Medium | Under about 120, report counts and stories rather than percentages. Decide that now, not after seeing the data |
| **Permission for names, quotes, photos** | 🟠 Medium | Ask clearly inside the conversation. Confirm it's in the flow for every route, including the calls |

## 9 · Half 2 — the report, and the four months after it

Done **30 Sep**. Then it works until February:

- **Oct** — the credibility story goes public. The headline is the build sheet's own line: **InvokED
  is not a two-day event** — the combined "something happened afterwards" number from questions 4
  and 5. Individual stories run as their own pieces.
- **Oct–Nov** — the **future readiness** piece: how 150–250 senior education leaders define it. This
  is the one with a life beyond InvokED, and the one we promised the people who answered.
- **Nov–Dec** — the **Forum and partnership leads**: everyone who said "I wanted to do something and
  couldn't". That's a warm list, not a failure list.
- **Dec–Feb** — feeds **InvokED 6.0** (26–27 Feb 2027): the proof paragraph in the invites, the
  story told from the stage, Awards nominations, and the list of people who connect others.

All of it stays bound by the five honesty rules — above all: **never report the question 4 "no" on
its own**, and **never make the shift in thinking sound like something we measured** rather than
something people told us.

## 10 · Open questions

1. **The name** — "What Happened Next", or one of the three others in §2a?
2. **Who builds the web form, and can it be live by 14 Aug?** Now more important than the bot.
3. **Is the MItra bot being built, by whom, and by when?**
4. **Do the three things we're offering back clear?** Especially early access to the report and the
   future readiness findings — both are promises we then have to keep.
5. **Who owns cleaning the contact list**, and what's the honest reachable number?
6. **Who does the ~30 calls?** That's roughly 10 hours plus scheduling. It's the highest-value work
   in the campaign and it needs a named person.
7. **Is 30 Sep the report to Sonal, or the report published?** Changes the last week a lot.
8. **Sonal's okay on the sender list** — Shibulal's name on ~150 personal notes is a real ask on his
   time.
