# Anita Sharma portrait — judged the interview, drafted both styles

## What got made
- **Critical read of the Anita Sharma interview** (SCERT Haryana, Subject Specialist in Physics;
  interviewer Bhavya Kukreja). Verdict: a story exists but the transcript cannot carry a
  full-length portrait yet.
- **Message to Bhavya** explaining why, and the plan ahead (pull the video, 25-min follow-up,
  9 questions). Questionnaire has gone to the concerned member; we're waiting on answers.
- **Two drafts written into the interview's own Google Doc** as new tabs:
  *Draft 1 - Interview* (edited Q&A) and *Draft 2 - Story* (narrative portrait,
  "Where Curiosity Finds Its Answer").

## What we learned
- The transcript's usable core is small but real: the class 7 reflection-of-light experiment
  (Shri Virendra Chaubey, a mirror and three pins), the B.Ed micro-teaching realisation, the
  household-material STEAM design so curiosity costs nothing, and monitoring e-content
  *consumption* rather than deployment.
- What it lacks is structural, not stylistic: no stakes, no numbers, nobody named, no place.
  An interview format tolerates those absences; a portrait does not. That's the real reason
  the story reads thin, and it's an interviewing gap rather than a subject gap.
- Never asked: she is a woman who chose physics and now runs ed-tech for a state department.
  Likely the strongest untapped vein.

## What to improve next
- New engine capability, permanent: **`gs.py doc-highlight`** colours every regex match
  (default `[[…]]`, yellow) inside a named tab. Doc *tab* bodies are inserted as plain text,
  so drafts previously had no way to mark their own gaps. Convention now documented in the
  gsuite skill + README: write `[[FILL: …]]` inline, then highlight. Load-bearing gaps only.
- Portrait stays in the Doc until Sonal approves; brain gets the approved version after.
