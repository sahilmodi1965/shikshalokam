# MItra blog Part 3 reframed around the four use cases, with everything named

Parts 1 (why it matters) and 2 (journey of building MItra) are published. Sonal handed over the
Part 3 questionnaire she had planned and asked for it to be revamped now that the use cases are
known.

## What got made
- **Part 3 tab rewritten in place** in the blog questionnaire Doc. Parts 1 and 2 tabs untouched.
  → https://docs.google.com/document/d/1pLXmcCcMD2-v_eThOZT2ioRunchx4DFoTe_tpet0EqM/edit

## The reframe
The original Part 3 was themed *"stories of transformation"* — 8 questions, all effectively about
**Story Capture**. That is one use case of four. Left as it was, Part 3 would have repeated Part 2
and the other three use cases would never have been told.

**Use cases are now the spine; stories are the material.** Seven sections: one per use case
(Discussion Capture, Story Capture, Evaluation Studies, Perception Capture), then Tech/Product,
Comms, and a set for everyone. Around 26 questions, each section naming its real bots, personas,
geographies and dates so contributors answer about specific work rather than in general.

This still honours Part 2's closing promise of *"stories from teachers, mothers and community
leaders"*, and it lets Part 3 answer what Parts 1 and 2 leave open: **what is this actually for.**

## The call Sonal made
**Name the deployments and partners.** No generic "a state in the east." The tab now names Bihar,
Karnataka, Nagaland, Odisha, Telangana and Delhi; WLC, PPPI, Shiksha Chaupal and Chavadi, Meena
Manch, APSWREIS. Named work is what makes the piece credible to a funder or a government partner
reading it.

## The correction that matters more
The first version of the tab carried a decision record, a note explaining what had changed and why,
a clearance tracker and a "what the blog needs" section. Sonal: *"dont write the logics and changes
done in the doc, mention it here in the terminal always. those documents should just carry the right
content."* All of it stripped; the tab now holds theme, use cases, filling instructions, sections
and questions, nothing else. Individual questions were de-editorialised too — several carried our
framing ("this is likely the sharpest finding") rather than just asking the thing.

**This was already a standing rule.** It was learned on 2026-07-27 about sheets and written into
that session's digest, then broken two days later in a Doc. Now recorded as a permanent memory
covering every deliverable, not just sheets: reasoning lives in chat and in the brain, never in the
artefact.

## What we learned
- **A questionnaire written before the product's shape was known will quietly narrow the piece.**
  The original Part 3 wasn't wrong when written — Story Capture was most of what existed. It just
  stopped matching the thing it was describing, and nobody would have noticed until the blog read
  thin.
- **Named work is the credibility.** APSWREIS adopting the Telangana PTM bot as their own Parent
  Feedback Bot is the single strongest proof point in the whole set, and it only lands if both
  parties are named.
- **The questions worth asking are the uncomfortable ones.** Three went in deliberately: what a
  study cost before MItra (the entire argument for Evaluation Studies, and we have no number);
  whether the impact assessment bot's evidence actually supported SL's micro-improvement
  hypothesis; and what we should be honest about as a limitation. The original asked for none of
  these.
